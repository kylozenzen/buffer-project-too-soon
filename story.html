01 · Why this exists

Your posts were written last week. The news happened this morning.

I’ve spent years running social media and PR. I’ve watched too many brands fail to read the room. It’s rarely carelessness. A promo gets written and scheduled days ahead, then something terrible happens and nobody remembers what’s sitting in the queue.

MondayPost written

“Our new cold brew is on fire 🔥” goes into the Buffer queue for Thursday.

Thursday · 8:00 AMNews breaks

A warehouse fire downtown forces nearby families to evacuate.

Thursday · 4:30 PMThe post goes out
✗ Screenshots. Apology. Meeting.✓ Too Soon? held it at 8:05.
Scheduling tools are great at sending posts on time. Nobody was checking whether the time was still right.

There’s a long list of famous examples: SpaghettiOs on Pearl Harbor Day, DiGiorno jumping into a hashtag it hadn’t read, Gap tweeting about a hurricane. Big brands with full teams still got it wrong. Small teams and agencies managing ten clients have even less time to read the news for every one. The Guide has all 7 case files.

02 · What it does

A small but mighty tool that reads the room. Or your queue.

Open it and you see two columns: today’s news on the left, your scheduled Buffer posts on the right. Every post gets a stamp and a plain-English reason.

Too soon

Names someone in tragic news, echoes a disaster (“on fire” next to a fire), ties a promo to a solemn day, or links to a page that’s dead or sold out.

Give it a look

Mentions a place under a weather alert, a developing story, or a tribute post on a solemn day. Probably fine; worth a human glance.

You’re good

Nothing in today’s news touches it. When the whole queue is clean, you get a “days without a possible incident” board.

Then you fix it right there: rewrite it with a live check as you type, push it back a day, or pull it to drafts. For a really bad day, Go dark pulls everything coming up to drafts in one tap and helps you write a holding statement. An optional AI second opinion handles the awkward cases the rules can’t judge.

03 · Built on PostIQ

Sign in with Buffer, reused instead of rebuilt.

PostIQ, my calendar companion for Buffer, already had a working “Sign in with Buffer.” For Too Soon? I pulled that connection layer out into shared files and gave it settings, so any of my apps can plug it in. Sign-in took an afternoon instead of a week.

PostIQ plans.

Too Soon?

js/shared/connection.js shared

The shared version takes three settings: a storage prefix (so two apps on one browser never mix tokens), the public clientId, and the tokenEndpoint. PostIQ uses prefix: 'postiq'; Too Soon? uses prefix: 'toosoon'.

How the sign-in works

  1. Tap “Sign in with Buffer.”The app creates a one-time secret (a PKCE code verifier), keeps it in the browser, and sends only its SHA-256 fingerprint to auth.buffer.com.
  2. Approve on Buffer’s own page.You never type your Buffer password into Too Soon?. Buffer shows exactly what the app is asking for: read posts, edit posts, read your account.
  3. Buffer sends you back with a code./auth/callback.html checks the code matches the request it started, then trades code + verifier for tokens through a tiny Netlify function.
  4. Tokens stay in your browser.No database and no accounts. The access token refreshes on its own using the refresh token. Disconnect deletes both.

Buffer gave me a public client for this app, so there’s no client secret, and that’s fine: PKCE is built for apps that can’t keep a secret. The client ID lives in a Netlify environment variable and reaches the page through /.netlify/functions/app-config. If a secret is ever issued, buffer-token adds it on the server and the browser never sees it. An API-key option is still there as a fallback.

The rules engine (js/rules.js, 255 lines)

When people are criticizing your brand on Bluesky or Reddit, every promo gets a “give it a look” until it cools down; criticism of a watch word flags the posts that mention it. Calm chatter is shown, never flagged.

The engine pulls the names, places, handles and hashtags out of each post, then compares them with the headlines. It knows the difference between “Jordan Vale” (a deliberate reference) and a word that happens to appear in a story. It catches tone echoes, like “killer deal” next to a shooting or “blowing up” next to an explosion. It also checks dates that will have passed by post time, places under alerts, sensitive days and broken links, plus tidy-ups like duplicates, character limits and missing alt text. A sensitivity dial (strict, normal, relaxed) can be set per client.

Allposts go through the free rules, in your browser
Flaggedposts can go to AI when you tap ✨ Ask AI
Youdecide. AI explains the risk and drafts a rewrite

The AI second opinion uses Claude Haiku or Gemini Flash. Bring your own key (Gemini has a free tier); it stays in your browser and goes out only with the one post you asked about, plus today’s headlines.

06 · The taste pass

Funny about posting. Never funny about the news.

An app about tragedy-adjacent posts can easily become tasteless itself. These are the calls I changed after stepping back, plus a couple of bugs that real data caught. Everyone also agrees to a short, plain-English note before they start: the news is real, the app is a second set of eyes and not the last, and the final call stays with a human.

BeforeThe demo’s fake news day had a mall shooting.
AfterA warehouse fire that forces families to evacuate. Still clearly bad timing for “on fire 🔥,” and nobody dies.
BeforeA sad trombone played whenever the counter reset.
AfterIt only plays for light causes, like a dead link or a passed date. Never when the cause is someone’s tragedy.
BeforeThe all-clear card could say “Heaviest story today” next to a smiling coffee mug.
AfterThe cheerful card never quotes tragic headlines or emergency alerts.
Bug, found in live dataA months-old GDACS drought alert listed the United States and Mexico, so “our Mexico City street tacos” got flagged.
FixedDroughts and anything older than 10 days are dropped. Alaska freezing-spray warnings and Wikipedia’s non-people (films, ships) are filtered too.
